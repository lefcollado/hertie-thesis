**Thesis supervisor:** Prof. Dr. Asya Magazinnik

**Abstract**  
This study examines the determinants of active mobility in the Metropolitan Region of São Paulo and investigates whether these determinants changed between 2017 and 2023. Using microdata from the Origin-Destination Survey, the analysis combines logistic regression models with interaction terms and Random Forest models interpreted through feature importance and SHAP values. The results indicate that active mobility remains primarily shaped by structural constraints such as distance, income, and access to private vehicles. While some changes are observed in the intensity of relationships, the overall structure of determinants remains stable over time. Machine learning results support these findings and reveal additional nonlinear patterns, reinforcing the dominant role of distance and the persistent influence of socioeconomic conditions. The study contributes to the literature with a perspective on mobility determinants over time in a large metropolitan region of the Global South and by integrating statistical and machine learning approaches. The findings suggest that changes in mobility behavior reflect adjustments within existing structural conditions rather than a transformation of underlying dynamics, highlighting the importance of addressing spatial inequalities and urban form in policy design.  
**Keywords:** active mobility; urban mobility; São Paulo; travel behavior; logistic regression; Random Forest; Global South
